## (Really) short bio

Steve Tonneau is a Reader (Associate Professor) at the University of Edinburgh, where he joined as a lecturer in 2019. He defended his PhD in 2015 after 3 years in the [INRIA/IRISA Mimetic](http://www.irisa.fr/mimetic/) research team, and pursued a post-doc in robotics at LAAS-CNRS in Toulouse, within the [Gepetto](http://projects.laas.fr/gepetto/index.php/) team.

His research focuses on motion planning based on the biomechanical analysis of motion invariants. Applications include computer graphics animation as well as robotics. He is a member of the [Institute of Perception, Action and Behaviour (IPAB)](https://informatics.ed.ac.uk/ipab) of the School of Informatics.

## Research interests

Legged robots and humanoids, contact planning and multi-contact locomotion, motion planning, model predictive control and optimal control, whole-body manipulation, character animation and computer graphics. See the [publications](/publications/) page for recent work and the [projects](/projects/) page for the main lines of work.

## Prospective students

I am deputy director of the UKRI AI Centre for Doctoral Training in Dependable and Deployable AI for Robotics ([CDT-D2AIR](https://www.cdt-d2air.uk/)), and the CDT is the way to work with me as a PhD student. The [supervision](/supervision/) page explains how to apply, and lists the students and post-docs I work or have worked with. You can also get in touch through the [contact](/contact/) page.
